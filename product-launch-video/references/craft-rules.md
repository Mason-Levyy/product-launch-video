# Launch video craft rules

Distilled from what founders and marketers report working on X, Product Hunt, LinkedIn and the
Remotion/Claude Code community (2026). Use this in Phase 3 when writing the hook and shot list.

## What the strongest launches have in common
- **Outcome over feature tour.** Open on the problem, show the result. A feature list asks the viewer
  to imagine the value; an outcome shows it.
- **The first 3–5 seconds decide it.** Drop-off is steepest there. Name the viewer's exact pain in the
  first line/frame. Caption, thumbnail and first frame must promise the same thing or it reads as bait.
- **Motion never dies.** Something changes every 1–2 seconds during the opening.
- **One complete task.** Show a real task start to finish (request → what the product does → result).
  For AI/agent products, make clear what's automatic and where a human approves.
- **A claim people can repeat.** One sentence, concrete, no category jargon.
- **Close on a measurable outcome** (time saved, count, before/after), then one CTA.
- **Silent-first.** Most feeds autoplay muted; text on screen carries the story.
- **Post + video together.** The written post (announcement → problem → what it does → CTA) matters
  as much as the video.
- **Ship several cuts at once:** 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, silent website hero, short teaser.

## Structure patterns (pick one)

### A. Problem → Solution sizzle (default, 20–45s)
1. Hook — pain as a question or blunt statement (3s)
2. Product intro — name + repeatable claim (3s)
3. Demo — the one core interaction, input → action → result (8–15s)
4. Payoff — result/before-after or 2–3 benefit lines (5s)
5. Proof — a real number or logo row (3s)
6. CTA — URL, one action (3s)

### B. Pure demo walkthrough (45s–2min)
Hook (3s) → "Here's how it works" → 2–3 steps of the real product with push-ins and callouts on each
key click → result → CTA. Best for tools where the UI *is* the proof (dev tools, productivity apps).

### C. Cinematic / hype (15–30s)
Bold claim or contrarian statement → fast montage of UI moments and text slams (cuts every ~1s) →
single reveal of the product name → CTA. Goes for reach on X; weakest on explaining.

### D. Founder-led
Founder to camera (why I built this, 10s) → screen demo (20–40s) → founder CTA. Use when the founder's
story or credibility is the hook. Overlay titles/captions in the empty space above the head.

## Hook formulas
- Pain question: "Still ___ by hand?"
- Cost of the status quo: "Your team loses 5 hours a week to ___."
- Contrarian: "___ is broken. So we rebuilt it."
- Before/after split: show the painful old way for 1.5s, hard cut to the new way.
- The result first: open on the finished output, then "made in 30 seconds with ___."

Keep hooks short enough to read in one glance (well under ten words on screen).

## Visual conventions that look "premium"
- Dark neutral or brand-tinted background with one accent color; subtle radial glow behind the hero.
- Big type, few words, lots of space. One idea per frame.
- Real UI, slightly zoomed and pushed in, beats a full-screen static screenshot.
- Cursor with a soft trail, click ripple, and a zoom toward the thing being clicked.
- Hard cuts or slides between beats; avoid stacked crossfades.
- Numbers count up (tabular-nums) instead of appearing.

## Anti-patterns
- Opening on the logo.
- Three generic feature cards ("Fast", "Secure", "Collaborative").
- Walls of text; captions that repeat the post word for word.
- Invented stats, fake testimonials, fake user counts. Only real proof — or none.
- Bouncy springs on text, linear slides, particle effects on every scene.
- A demo that jumps between five features without finishing one task.
